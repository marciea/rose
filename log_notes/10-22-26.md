> Placed above the applicable general LAN allow rule. 
> - Direction is measured at the firewall interface. 

> An access port can serve an ordinary untagged computer. PVID classifies arriving untagged frames; tagged/untagged membership determines their presentation when leaving a port.
> One trunk can carry multiple VLANs while having only one PVID.
## Troubleshooting and actual cutover

1. OPNsense initially had LAN assigned to plain `em0` at `192.168.1.1`. The switch already sent VLAN 10 and 20 tagged on port 1. Those settings did not provide the intended GUI path.
2. A direct computer-to-Tiny Ethernet connection showed `no carrier` and no link LEDs. Both devices established a physical link when connected to the switch. The direct-link fault was bypassed, not diagnosed to a root cause.
3. The switch configuration was backed up to the MacBook. VLAN mode was temporarily disabled (which it should not have been in the first place) . OPNsense LAN was changed to `192.168.20.1`, and the computer to `192.168.20.2`. The flat, untagged switch path restored GUI access.
4. OPNsense VLAN devices, assignments and firewall policy were discussed and prepared. Moving LAN to VLAN 10 was explicitly corrected: LAN belongs to VLAN 20.
5. The owner reports reconnecting port 2 while hosts were down and DNS/DHCP services were disabled, preparing OPNsense, and applying the saved switch configuration.
6. Internet worked on the PC after a reboot. The reason the reboot was needed was not established. Stale client configuration is a possibility, not a confirmed diagnosis.
7. Ping from `192.168.20.2` to `192.168.1.10` failed. This is consistent with the intended block but is not sufficient evidence that the firewall caused it.
